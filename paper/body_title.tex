\title[Detecting text-reading hallucinations for blind users]{Detecting Text-Reading Hallucinations in Vision-Language Descriptions of Cyrillic Signage, Packaging and Documents for Blind Users}

\abstract{Blind people increasingly rely on vision-language models to read signs, product labels and documents aloud, but a model that cannot read a word tends to produce a plausible one instead. We measure how often this happens for Ukrainian (Cyrillic) text with four Claude models of one vendor, and whether it can be detected at run time. Study~1 uses \NImages{} synthetic images in a factorial design that crosses image quality, framing (cropping, finger or glare occlusion) and the type of the distinctive name (real word, invented word, near-word). Study~2 uses \RealNImages{} real images, namely Open Food Facts packaging photographs and official Ukrainian documents, with ground truth independent of the evaluated models. Only \RangeHall\% (Study~1) and \RealRangeHall\% (Study~2) of quoted words were hallucinated. An automated audit confirmed most hallucination labels in Study~1 but only about half in Study~2, where the reference text misses some printed content. The audit-adjusted rates are therefore \SynAdjRange\% and \RealAdjRange\%. Yet \RangeImg\% and \RealRangeImg\% of descriptions contained at least one word labelled as hallucinated, mostly without hedging. In a paired probe on real documents, a printed near-word was reported as the real word it resembles in \RangePrNorm\% of pages. Within the family, the most expensive model was not the most faithful reader. Combining the model's own re-reading, an independent OCR pass and agreement between models detected hallucinated words with an AUROC of \RangeAucComb{} (Study~1) and \RealRangeAucComb{} (Study~2). We report paired tests, run-to-run stability, cost and latency, and an audit of the labeller by two automated raters, and we derive design recommendations for assistive reading applications.}

\keywords{blind and visually impaired users, vision-language models, hallucination detection, scene text reading, Cyrillic script, assistive technology}
